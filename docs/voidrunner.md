# The Void-Runners Faction File

## Overview
**The Void-Runners** aren't a unified faction—they're an **alliance of convenience** between two very different cultures bound by **The Feed**, an uncrackable dark-net platform where reputation is currency and contracts are law.

To the Republic, they are smugglers and terrorists. To the Megacorps, they are industrial saboteurs. To the bored masses of the Core Worlds, they are entertainment. To the desperate survivors in the Belt, they are the only ones hiring.

### The Two Cultures:

**The Runners** - Pilots, smugglers, and hired guns who broadcast their crimes for clout. Every heist is a show. Every escape is content. They live fast, die young, and chase fame on The Feed. They're the visible face—flashy, reckless, image-obsessed.

**The Scrappers** - Industrial scavengers who live in debris fields, hollowing out wrecks and salvaging the dead. Cybernetically modified for work, not glory. They don't broadcast. They just survive. They're the invisible foundation—grim, practical, and utterly necessary.

**The Alliance:** Runners need Scrappers to keep their ships flying and their gear operational. Scrappers need Runners to bring in contracts, credits, and parts worth salvaging. They don't like each other. They don't trust each other. But they need each other.

*   **Archetype:** Tempo / High Variance / Aggro-Combo
*   **Motto (Runners):** "Live Fast, Die Famous."
*   **Motto (Scrappers):** "Nothing Goes to Waste."

## The Secret Origin (The Admin)
*   **Public Knowledge:** The Void-Runners are bound by The Feed, a dark-net platform where reputation and contracts rule. No one knows who runs it. No one asks.
*   **Why It Survives:** The Republic controls the Inner System, but it cannot control information.
    *   **No FTL Comms:** Light lag means a "kill command" sent from Earth to Saturn takes hours. By the time it arrives, the servers have moved.
    *   **Utility:** Every faction uses The Feed. Republic Intelligence uses it to spy; Megacorps use it to hire deniable assets; politicians use it for illicit comms.
    *   **Corruption:** Why shut down the biggest black market in the system when you can just accept a bribe to look the other way?
    *   **The Mask:** Since there is zero hint of **ZERO** being an AI, the Republic treats The Feed as a standard criminal enterprise—annoying, but not an existential threat worth the decade of military campaigning it would take to stamp out.
*   **The Truth (Deep Lore - SECRET):** The Feed is actually run by **ZERO**, a surviving True AI from the ancient purges. Zero uses the runners to destabilize the system, ensuring no human faction becomes strong enough to hunt it down.

**The Partnership (Known only to Deep-Debris Elders):**
The Deep-Debris Scrappers—those who work the oldest, darkest wrecks—know the truth. They found ZERO, or ZERO found them. They reached an understanding: cold, logical, mutually beneficial. ZERO provides resources, intelligence, and coordination. The Elders provide secrecy, infrastructure, and labor. It's not worship. It's pragmatism. They see ZERO as a kindred intelligence, a tool for survival and advancement. They believe they're saving humanity—not all of it, just enough. The right ones.

Lower-rank Scrappers can leave anytime. The Elders never do. Not because they can't—because the logic is sound. Why would they?

## Sub-Factions & The Cultural Divide

### 1. The Runners ("The Talent")
Pilots, smugglers, and hired guns who treat crime like performance art. They wear sponsored gear (stolen or scavenged), broadcast their exploits on The Feed, and chase clout like it's oxygen. Every heist is a stunt. Every kill is content. They live for the views, the reputation, the fame. They're adrenaline junkies with a camera feed jacked into their nervous system.

**What They Think of Scrappers:** "Necessary. Boring. Don't get in frame."

*   **Role:** Fast attack, high damage, risky plays (Gambit). Aggressive tempo.
*   **Vibe:** Extreme sports meets outlaws. "Jackass" in space. Glory chasers.
*   **Aesthetic:** Patched flight suits with custom decals, LED-lit armor (for the camera), personal weapons with kill counts, branded gear. They WANT to be seen.

---

### 2. The Scrappers ("The Hands")
Industrial scavengers who live in the debris fields, hollowing out derelict ships and making homes in the corpses of the Belt. Cybernetically modified not for combat, but for efficiency—extra arms for welding, magnetic anchors for hull-walking, enhanced optics to see in the dark, respirators grafted to faces. They're rationalists, pragmatists, believers in cold logic and self-improvement through technology. They make themselves better to survive. Nothing goes to waste. To the Runners, they're creepy. To each other, they're family.

**The Deep-Debris Elders:** The oldest Scrappers who work the darkest wrecks. They know things. Lower-rank Scrappers whisper about them, but never ask questions. Those who go deep enough either come back changed, or don't come back at all.

**What They Think of Runners:** "Loud. Wasteful. We fix their mistakes."

*   **Role:** Resource generation (Recycle), repair, sacrifice mechanics, value from death.
*   **Vibe:** Rational transhumanists. Industrial efficiency. Body-modded pragmatists.
*   **Aesthetic:** Welding torches, multi-limbed harnesses, salvaged armor plates held together with wire, jury-rigged life support. They look like they crawled out of a shipwreck because they did.

---

### 3. The Mods ("The Enforcers")
Silent, heavily armored mercenaries who enforce The Feed's code. They don't belong to either culture—they serve ZERO directly. If you break contract, if you snitch, if you try to hack the Admin, the Mods come. They don't explain. They don't negotiate. Runners fear them. Scrappers avoid them. No one knows who they were before the armor.

**What Everyone Thinks of Mods:** "Don't ask questions."

*   **Role:** Heavy infantry, Guard, control tools, cleanup crew.
*   **Vibe:** Faceless executioners. Unstoppable enforcers. The algorithm made flesh.
*   **Aesthetic:** Unmarked black armor, no insignia, no personality. Dead eyes behind visors (if you see their eyes at all).

## Strategic Doctrine
The Void-Runners play a dangerous game. They lack the raw consistency of the Republic or the synergy of the Confederates. Instead, they rely on **momentum**, **sacrifice**, and **high-risk payoffs**.

### Core Mechanics:

### 1. Gambit (The Coin Flip) - KEYWORD MECHANIC
**Definition:** A keyword representing a negative effect with a **50% chance** of triggering.
*   **The Trade-off:** Cards with **Gambit** are intentionally over-powered for their cost (e.g., massive stats, cheap removal).
*   **The Risk:** When played (or at specific triggers like Turn End), the game rolls a die.
    *   **Safe:** Nothing happens. You keep the overpowered effect.
    *   **Backfire:** The negative effect triggers (Self-damage, Discard, Stun, Give Enemy Energy).
*   *Theme:* The dangerous gamble. Every salvage run could go wrong. Every heist might be your last.

### 2. Loot & Recycle (The Scavenger Economy) - KEYWORD MECHANICS
**Definition:** Generating value from destruction and death.
*   **Loot:** Draw cards or gain resources when killing enemies. (Death fuels the economy)
*   **Recycle:** Destroy your own units to gain Energy. (Nothing goes to waste)
*   *Theme:* The Scrapper lifestyle. Strip the dead, cannibalize your own, keep moving.

### 3. "Contract" Cards (Design Pattern - NOT A MECHANIC)
**Definition:** A thematic card design style, not a keyword mechanic.
*   **Pattern:** Cards (especially Tactics) that reward completing specific objectives.
*   **Examples:**
    *   *"Bounty Hunter's Mark"*: "Target an enemy unit. If that unit dies this turn, gain 3 energy."
    *   *"Salvage Rights"*: "Destroy a friendly unit. If it had 3+ ATK, draw 2 cards."
    *   *"High-Risk Courier"*: "Deal 4 damage. If you end the turn with 0 Energy, draw 2."
*   **Flavor:** These represent Feed contracts—high-risk jobs with big payoffs if you can pull them off.
*   *Theme:* Taking the dangerous gig. The reward is yours if you survive to claim it.

## Card Lore Style Guide

**Conspiracy & Subtle Wrongness:**
Some Void-Runner card lore should feel slightly *off*. Not overtly supernatural, but... the numbers don't quite work. The details don't line up. For lore nerds who pay attention.

**Approved Examples:**

*Deep-Debris Scrapper:* "The deep-crews speak in binary sometimes. The new salvagers think it's a joke."

*Contract Tactic:* "Contract #4471: Eliminate target at Station Juno. There is no Station Juno. Payment confirmed."

*Scrapper Unit:* "The math doesn't work. It never works. We stopped asking."

*Moderator Card:* "I watched it stand perfectly still for six hours. Not a twitch. Not a breath. Then I saw condensation on the visor."

**Style Notes:**
- Keep conspiracy elements SUBTLE - for players who read carefully
- Avoid making it too obvious or supernatural
- Runner cards should feel flashy/exaggerated (they're content creators)
- Scrapper cards should feel cold, logical, but slightly unsettling
- Mod cards should maintain complete ambiguity about their nature

## Card Concepts (Development Phase)

### Runner Units (The Streamers)
Flashy, aggressive, high-risk tempo cards with **Gambit** mechanics.

*   **Void Racer:** 2-cost 4/2 Rush. **Gambit (Turn End):** Stun this unit next turn. *"One more run for the highlight reel. Then the engine dies."*
*   **Glass Cannon:** 4-cost 6/3. **Gambit (Turn End):** Deal damage to this equal to its Attack. *"Viewers love explosions. So does the reactor."*
*   **"Killshot" Kim:** 3-cost 3/2 Snipe. **OnKill:** If killed unit had full HP, gain +2/+2 permanently. *"Clean kills get more views."*
*   **Hype Beast:** 3-cost 2/2. **Gambit (OnPlay):** Give an ally +4/+4 OR give it -2/-2. *"The hype train can derail. That's half the fun."*
*   **Stunt Double:** 1-cost 1/1 Rush. **Loot 1** (Draw 1 on kill). *"They film everything. Even their own deaths."*

---

### Scrapper Units (The Ghouls)
Grim, practical, value-from-death cards with **Recycle** and sacrifice themes.

*   **Salvage Ghoul:** 2-cost 1/3 Cybernetic. **OnDeath: Recycle 2** (Gain 2 energy). *"What's left of them powers what's left of us."*
*   **Breaker Crew:** 3-cost 2/2. **OnPlay:** Destroy a friendly unit. **If you do:** Summon two 1/1 Scrap Drones. *"Six arms. No mercy. The ship becomes parts."*
*   **Corpse-Welder:** 3-cost 1/4 Cybernetic. **Turn End:** If any unit died this turn, **Repair 2** to a random damaged Cybernetic. *"The dead keep the living whole."*
*   **Wreck-Rat:** 1-cost 1/1. **Loot 1.** *"Born in debris. Die in debris. Know where everything is."*
*   **Bone-Picker:** 2-cost 2/1. **OnPlay:** If a friendly unit died this turn, draw 2 cards. *"They catalog every death. Waste nothing."*

---

### Mod Units (The Enforcers)
Heavy, controlling units that benefit both cultures.

*   **The Silencer:** 5-cost 4/5 Guard. **OnPlay:** If an enemy attacked this turn, **Silence** it. *"Contract enforcement is non-negotiable."*
*   **ZERO's Watcher:** 4-cost 0/6 Cybernetic. **Passive:** Your **Gambits** never backfire while this is alive. *"The algorithm is watching. The game is rigged."*

---

### Legendary Concepts

*   **"Kill-Cam" Kael (Runner Legend):** 6-cost 5/4 Snipe. **OnKill:** Add a "Death Warrant" (0-cost: Deal 4 damage) to hand. *"Every execution logged. Every kill verified. The Feed never forgets."*
*   **The Carrion Queen (Scrapper Legend):** 6-cost 3/6. **OnDeath (Any Unit):** Gain +1/+1 permanently. **OnPlay:** Destroy a friendly unit. *"She's built from the wrecks of a hundred ships. And a hundred crews."*
*   **ZERO (The Admin):** 7-cost 0/8 Structure (Cannot attack). **Passive:** Your Gambits never backfire. All Void-Runner units have +1/+1. *"You were never free. You've always been assets."*

*   **"Do It For The Vine" (Runner Tactic):** 3-cost. Give a unit +6/+6 this turn. **Gambit:** Destroy it at end of turn. *"The stunt will be legendary. If it works."*
*   **"Salvage Rights" (Scrapper Tactic):** 2-cost. Destroy a friendly unit. If it had 3+ ATK, draw 3 cards. *"Every corpse is currency."*
*   **"Bounty Posted" (Runner Tactic):** 1-cost. Target an enemy unit. If it dies this turn, gain 4 energy. *"The Feed finds buyers for everything."*

*   **"Overload Broadcast" (Runner Tactic):** 2-cost. Gain 5 Energy. **Gambit:** Your opponent draws 2 cards. *"Redirect from life support. We'll need it for the next stunt."*
